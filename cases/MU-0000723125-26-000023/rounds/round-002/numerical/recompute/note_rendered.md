## Key points

- **Reported:** revenue was $54.2bn in FY2026 Q4, up 30.8% sequentially and 379.3% year on year, with a gross margin of 86.8% and an operating margin of 80.7%. FY2026 was a long year, and by our count from the reported period end dates the extra week fell in this quarter, which flatters both comparisons: “Fiscal 2026 contained 53 weeks and fiscal 2025 and 2024 each contained 52 weeks.”
- **Guidance (reported):** for the first quarter of FY2027 management guides revenue of “$61.5 billion ± $1.5 billion” on both a GAAP and a non-GAAP basis ($61.5bn at the midpoint). Gross margin guidance is “Approximately 85.95%” on a GAAP basis and “Approximately 86.25%” on a non-GAAP basis. Diluted EPS guidance is “$37.84 ± $1.00” on a GAAP basis and “$38.15 ± $1.00” on a non-GAAP basis.
- **Our forecast:** FY2027Q1 revenue of $61.5bn and EPS of $37.50; next-twelve-month revenue of $269.5bn and EPS of $163.56.
- **Valuation and rating:** our target price of $1,102 implies 6.4% upside to the 2026-10-08 close of $1,035.84, inside the Neutral band. We rate the shares **Neutral**.
- **Our view:** demand is strong and pricing is supported by new customer agreements, but the shares already discount much of the upswing, and our model assumes the memory cycle normalizes after FY2027 in the outer years of the DCF.

- “The AI-driven growth in the data center has accelerated demand for memory and storage at a rate greater than our ability and the industry’s ability to increase supply.”

## Model and valuation

**Our forecast (model assumptions, not company guidance):**

- FY2027Q1 revenue of $61.5bn is anchored on the guidance midpoint of $61.5bn. Sequential growth then slows through the rest of FY2027 (our view).
- Gross margin is held flat at the GAAP guidance of “Approximately 85.95%” for all four forecast quarters. Operating expenses are set at the guided GAAP ratio to revenue (“Approximately $2.31 billion” on the revenue midpoint), and the tax rate is the FY2026 effective rate, which reflects Pillar Two (“The change in our effective tax rate for 2026 as compared to 2025 was primarily due to the 15% minimum tax Pillar Two Model Rules (“Pillar Two”).”).
- FY2027Q1 EPS of $37.50 sits just below the GAAP EPS guidance midpoint of $37.84, mainly because the model excludes interest income.
- Next-twelve-month revenue is $269.5bn and EPS is $163.56.

**Valuation (code output from our inputs):**

- The discounted cash flow value is $862.83 per share. The terminal value is 56% of enterprise value. The DCF assumes a cycle peak followed by revenue normalization and declining free-cash-flow margins in the outer years (our view).
- The peer cross-check uses a median EV/Sales multiple of 15.1x for a group of fab-owning and wafer-fab-equipment companies. None of them is a memory maker, so we give this method the minority weight and the DCF the majority weight.
- Net cash is $68.3bn: cash and cash equivalents plus short- and long-term marketable investments, less total debt (code output). Both valuation methods add this net cash to reach equity value per share.
- The blended target price is **$1,102**, 6.4% above the 2026-10-08 close of $1,035.84. That is below the Outperform threshold of +15% and above the Underperform threshold of -10%, so the rating is **Neutral**.

**Valuation caveat (our view, analyst judgment):** refundable customer deposits received under the strategic customer agreements are recorded as noncurrent customer contract liabilities. We treat them as operating liabilities and do not deduct them as debt in net cash. The 10-K classifies them as contract liabilities rather than debt, and a deposit is returned only if the customer meets its minimum purchase commitments. However, the cash received sits inside reported cash, the 10-K says nearly all of the deposits are scheduled to be repaid between 2029 and 2031, and our free-cash-flow path does not include that repayment. Treating the deposits as debt-like would therefore lower our valuation modestly; we have not computed that alternative in code.

## Risks

- **Cycle risk (our view):** memory pricing has historically mean-reverted after supply-constrained peaks. If supply catches up sooner than we model, revenue and margins could fall faster than in our DCF. If tightness lasts longer, our outer-year normalization is too conservative.
- **Trade and export controls:** “International trade disputes, geopolitical tensions, and military conflicts have led, and continue to lead, to new and increasing export restrictions, trade barriers, tariffs, and other measures, as well as retaliatory actions, that can increase our manufacturing costs, make our products less competitive, reduce demand for our products, limit our ability to sell to certain customers or markets, limit our ability to procure or increase our costs for components or raw materials, impede or slow the movement of our goods across borders, impede our ability to perform R&D activities, or otherwise restrict our ability to conduct operations.” We do not model this risk separately.
- **Comparability:** the extra week in FY2026 Q4 (“Fiscal 2026 contained 53 weeks and fiscal 2025 and 2024 each contained 52 weeks.”) distorts growth rates and the trailing base used for the EV/Sales cross-check.
- **Valuation inputs:** the discount rate and terminal assumptions are analyst judgments. The snapshot has no market-implied inputs to calibrate them. The peer group contains no memory makers. The treatment of customer deposits described above is also a judgment that favours the shares.
- **Data gaps:** the 10-K XBRL does not tag a fourth-quarter diluted share count or diluted EPS. The share count used in the model is the latest tagged quarterly figure.
