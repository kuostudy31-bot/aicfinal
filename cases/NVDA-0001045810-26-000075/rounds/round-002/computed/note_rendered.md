# NVDA FY2027 Q2 earnings update: Underperform, target price $182

Fiscal quarter FY2027 Q2 (calendar equivalent CY2026Q2), quarter ended 2026-07-26. Labels used below: **Reported** = figures from the company's SEC XBRL data or filing text; **Forecast** = our model output from stated assumptions; **Judgment** = the analyst's opinion.

## Key points

- **Reported:** Revenue was $96.2bn, up 105.9% year on year and 17.9% sequentially, with gross margin of 75.0% and diluted EPS of $2.46.
- **Reported (guidance):** Management guides next-quarter revenue to $108.0bn: “Revenue is expected to be $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%.” Gross margin is guided down to 74.0%, and China Data Center compute revenue is excluded from the outlook (none assumed).
- **Forecast:** We model FY2027Q3 revenue of $108.0bn and EPS of $2.43; next-twelve-month revenue is $492.4bn and EPS $11.12.
- **Valuation (code output):** At the last close of $209.66, our blended target price of $182 implies -13.2% upside, which falls in the Underperform band.
- **Judgment:** The operating results are strong; the rating reflects valuation, not a call on the quarter's quality. The rating is sensitive to the discount rate and to the treatment of marketable securities (see Model and valuation).

## Results

**Reported.** Revenue for FY2027 Q2 was $96.2bn, up 105.9% from the year-ago quarter and 17.9% from the prior quarter. Trailing-twelve-month revenue reached $303.0bn. Data Center remained the driver: “Data Center revenue for the second quarter was a record $89.0 billion, up 117% from a year ago and up 18% sequentially, driven by the ramp of our Blackwell Ultra infrastructure.”

Gross margin was 75.0% and operating margin 66.2%. The company attributes the stable gross margin to product mix: “GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins were approximately flat sequentially as our Blackwell architecture remains the vast majority of our revenue.” Net income was $59.7bn and diluted EPS $2.46. Free cash flow (operating cash flow less capital expenditure, derived by code) was $21.4bn for the quarter.

On capital return, “During the second quarter of fiscal 2027, NVIDIA returned approximately $26.0 billion to shareholders in the form of shares repurchased and cash dividends.” On supply, the company disclosed a large step-up in purchase obligations: “Our commitments increased from $119 billion last quarter to $279 billion, primarily related to the procurement of memory.”

## Guidance and outlook

**Reported (company guidance for FY2027Q3).**

- Revenue: “Revenue is expected to be $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%.”
- Gross margin: “GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins are expected to be 74.0%, plus or minus 50 basis points.”
- Operating expenses: “GAAP and non-GAAP operating expenses are expected to be approximately $9.2 billion and $9.0 billion, respectively.”
- Tax rate: “For the full year fiscal 2027, NVIDIA expects GAAP and non-GAAP tax rates to be between 16.0% and 18.0%, excluding any discrete items and material changes to NVIDIA’s tax environment.”
- China: “NVIDIA is not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in its outlook.”

Management's demand commentary was emphatic: “And demand is accelerating.”

**Forecast.** Our FY2027Q3 revenue forecast of $108.0bn is anchored on the guidance midpoint of $108.0bn, and we use the guided gross margin of 74.0%, GAAP operating expenses near $9.2bn (non-GAAP $9.0bn) and a tax rate of 17.0%, the midpoint of the full-year range. Our FY2027Q3 EPS forecast is $2.43.

**Judgment.** Beyond the guided quarter we assume sequential revenue growth decelerates from the recent pace, and we hold gross margin at the guided level rather than assuming a return to the FY2027 Q2 level, because the rise in commitments is mainly for memory procurement and may pressure costs. No China Data Center compute revenue is modelled, so a resumption of China shipments would be upside to our estimates.

## Model and valuation

**Forecast.** Next-twelve-month revenue is $492.4bn and EPS $11.12, built from four quarterly assumptions starting with FY2027Q3.

**Valuation (code output from our inputs).** The target price of $182 blends a discounted cash flow value of $136.46 per share, which carries the larger weight, with a peer EV/Sales cross-check at a peer median multiple of 23.0x (peers: AMD, AVGO, MRVL). The peer trailing-twelve-month windows end in May-June 2026 (AVGO and MRVL in May, AMD in June), against July 2026 for NVDA, so the peer multiples rest on slightly older periods. The terminal value accounts for 71% of DCF enterprise value. Net cash used in the bridge is $-10.9bn. Against the last close of $209.66, implied upside is -13.2%, giving a rating of Underperform.

**Judgment.** The DCF free-cash-flow margins are grounded in historical cash conversion, with the near term held below the multi-year average because receivables and inventory have absorbed cash.

**Judgment (net cash).** The net cash figure of $-10.9bn deducts total debt (short-term plus long-term debt) from mapped cash and cash equivalents only. It understates liquidity because no XBRL concept in the case snapshot carries the company's marketable securities, so code cannot include them. The filing states: “As of July 26, 2026, we had $56.6 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable debt securities as well as $42.8 billion of marketable equity securities.” The balance sheet lines are “Marketable debt securities | 34,143 | | | 39,065” and “Marketable equity securities | 42,783 | | | 12,886” (in millions, current period first). Some of the company's investments carry lock-up restrictions: “(1) Included $36.9 billion of investments that are subject to short-term lock-up restrictions on the ability to sell.” The published target price of $182 does not include these items. In the analyst's offline check, using the same valuation method, adding the marketable debt securities at full value and the marketable equity securities (either with the analyst's haircut for lock-ups, volatility and tax on unrealised gains, or at full value) raises the target only slightly. Upside stays below minus ten percent in every case, so the rating remains Underperform, but the margin to the Neutral band edge narrows to about two percentage points at most.

**Judgment (discount rate).** The rating is also sensitive to the discount rate. At a WACC roughly half a percentage point lower than ours (about a third to a half of a percentage point, depending on how net cash is treated), upside would cross into the Neutral band. We use the unrounded CAPM-based discount rate rather than a rounded-down figure.

## Risks

**Judgment.** Upside risks to our Underperform view: demand could stay stronger for longer than our decelerating growth path (“And demand is accelerating.”); China Data Center compute revenue, excluded from guidance (none assumed), could resume; and gross margin could hold at the FY2027 Q2 level of 75.0% rather than the guided 74.0%. Downside risks: concentration of revenue in hyperscaler AI-infrastructure spending, which is cyclical; competition from AMD GPUs and custom accelerators at AVGO and MRVL; cost pressure and inventory risk from much larger supply commitments (“Our commitments increased from $119 billion last quarter to $279 billion, primarily related to the procurement of memory.”); and working-capital drag from extended customer payment terms. Valuation is sensitive to the discount rate (a cut of roughly half a percentage point or less would move the rating to Neutral), to the treatment of marketable securities and to the terminal assumptions, which carry 71% of DCF enterprise value.
